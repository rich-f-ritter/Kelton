# Deep dive — Other Income & Expense differences: Kelton vs Seasons

T12 basis (Kelton ÷ 240 units, Seasons ÷ 440). Source: each model's `Income & Expense Analysis` GL detail. **Big picture: once you classify the two statements consistently, the "other income" gap that drove the NOI bridge largely disappears — the real NOI/unit gap is an EXPENSE story, plus classification.**

## A. Other income — all of it (bundle, parking/storage, RUBS, fees)

| Group | Kelton T12 | K /unit | Seasons T12 | S /unit | Δ /unit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Resident bundle** (Kelton = TV/Cable "Revenue Share"; Seasons = "Concierge Package") | 290,428 | **1,210** | 514,872 | **1,170** | −40 |
| **Parking / storage / garage** | 99,060 | 413 | 380,815 | 865 | +452 |
| **RUBS / utility recovery** | 352,615 | 1,469 | 454,556 | 1,033 | −436 |
| **Fees & misc** (late, admin, pet, app, lease buyout/init, renters-liability, short-term, credit-report, maint/cleaning rev…) | 157,669 | 657 | 478,057 | 1,086 | +429 |
| **Total ancillary income** | **899,771** | **3,749** | **1,828,300** | **4,155** | **+406** |

**What this shows:**
1. **Both bundle a ~$1,200/unit resident package — it's just booked differently.** Kelton's is **TV/Cable Revenue Share ($1,210/u)**; Seasons' is **"Concierge Package Revenue" $514,872 ($1,170/u)** sitting inside misc Other Income. That single line is what made Seasons' "misc other income" look 3× Kelton's. Like-for-like it's a **wash (−$40/u)** — not a Seasons advantage.
2. **Storage/garage vs RUBS roughly offset.** Seasons earns **+$452/u** more on parking/storage (almost all `Storage/Garage Revenue $379,415`), while Kelton recovers **+$436/u** more RUBS (`RUBS-Water/Sewer/Trash $328,579`; Seasons also bills back electric/gas but nets lower/unit). These nearly cancel.
3. **The only real income edge is fees (+$429/u to Seasons).** Seasons runs **many more fee streams** (23 lines vs Kelton's 14): Lease Buy Out $74k, Lease Initiation $60k, Renters Liability $52k, Short-Term $39k, Credit Reporting $35k, Maintenance/Cleaning Revenue $64k, Background $24k, Furniture $14k… Kelton mostly has late ($28k) + admin ($22k) + pet ($21k). Seasons is a **more aggressively fee-monetized operation.**
4. **Net ancillary difference is only +$406/unit** — a fraction of what the rolled-up "misc other income" line implied.

### The flags (in order of size)
1. **Kelton G&A `61140 DUES & SUBSCRIPTIONS = $111,506` ($465/unit).** This is **77% of Kelton's entire G&A** and Seasons' equivalent is **$64**. $465/unit of dues is ~20–90× a normal apartment community. **Almost certainly a misclassification or corporate/software allocation** — scrub it. Reclassing or removing it swings ~$465/unit of NOI (≈ a third of the whole NOI/unit gap) and, at the 4.75% cap, ~$2.3M of value on 240 units.
2. **Turnover is classification, not efficiency.** Kelton books make-ready (Replacement Carpet $34k, Interior Paint $25k, Interior Cleaning $30k) in **Turnover ($431/u)**; Seasons books almost none there ($48/u) — its turn/make-ready sits in R&M or capex. Normalize before comparing; the true make-ready gap is far smaller than $383/u.
3. **Media/internet bundle expense.** Kelton `TV/Internet/Phone $185,201` ($772/u) vs Seasons `Internet Expense $82,625` ($188/u). Kelton's bundle costs more but **earns more** ($1,210/u cable income) — net the two and the media program is a **~+$450/u NOI positive** for Kelton, not a drag.
4. **Advertising / Internet Marketing** — Kelton `63110 INTERNET MARKETING $84,804` ($353/u) vs Seasons marketing ~$198/u.
5. **Contracts** — Kelton higher (+$202/u): Landscaping $43k, Refuse $46k, Valet Trash $15k, Security $18k. Some overlaps Seasons' Contracts (Garbage $93k, Landscape $43k) booked differently.
6. **Seasons runs higher Payroll (+$261/u) and Management (+$169/u)** — a larger 440-unit property with a concierge/services program staffs up more.

## C. Reconciliation & takeaways
- **The NOI/unit gap is not an "other income" story.** Matching Kelton's cable bundle to Seasons' concierge, and Kelton's RUBS to Seasons' storage/garage, ancillary income is ~a wash (+$406/u). Seasons' only real income edge is **fee monetization (+$429/u)**.
- **The gap is mostly EXPENSE + classification on Kelton's side**, led by the **$111,506 Dues & Subscriptions anomaly ($465/u)**, turnover-vs-R&M classification (~$383/u, mostly optical), advertising, and contracts — partly offset by Seasons' higher payroll/management.
- **Diligence list:**
  - **Kelton:** scrub `61140 Dues & Subscriptions $111,506` (what is it? reclassify); normalize Turnover vs R&M/capex; confirm the media bundle net economics.
  - **Seasons:** verify the **`Concierge Package Revenue $514,872`** is real, mandatory, and net-of-cost (does it carry offsetting payroll/contract cost?); verify **`Storage/Garage Revenue $379,415`** is sustainable and not double-counted with base rent; and that the **fee stack** (lease buyout, initiation, renters-liability, short-term) is normalized, not peak.
- Corrected read: the two assets earn **similar rent and similar net ancillary income**; Seasons shows a higher NOI/unit largely because **Kelton's opex is inflated by a probable G&A misclassification and turnover-classification differences**, with a genuine (smaller) fee-income edge to Seasons. Clean both statements to the same basis and the NOI/unit gap narrows materially.
